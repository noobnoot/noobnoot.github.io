Introduction to Mathematical Modeling

Mathematical modeling is the process of translating a real-world problem into mathematics to make predictions or decisions (Blum and Niss, 1991).

In a nutshell, mathematical modeling helps us solve real-life problems using techniques and strategies we learn in mathematics. For our scope, we focus on modeling quadratic equations.

The next section will provide detailed instructions and an example of an investigation paper.

Instructions

Your task is to create an investigation paper in which you will need to model an applicable real-life situation into a quadratic equation. Then, you must pose at least two (2) investigation questions and answer them objectively. A presentation will be held sometime before the First Term ends.

Paper Structure

Your investigation paper should include the following sections:

  1. Title: Provide a clear and concise title for your investigation. Include the names of all group members, section, teacher, and date submitted.

    Remember to consult with your teacher first about your title before finalizing it.

  2. Introduction: Describe the situation. Why is the problem important? Can it be modeled by a quadratic equation? Use appropriate research writing strategies and cite sources using APA 7 format.

    Try to limit this section to approximately one page only.

  3. Investigation Questions: Clearly state the problem being investigated. Formulate at least two (2) investigation questions that will serve as the main goal of the study.

    This investigation seeks to answer the following questions:

    • What is the quadratic equation that models the height (in meters) of the basketball?
    • Using the model, what is the maximum height reached by the basketball, and at what time does it occur?
  4. Assumptions: Identify the assumptions considered in constructing the model. Discuss possible limitations, constraints, or factors that may affect the accuracy or applicability of the investigation.
  5. Data collection: You may gather data from (1) actual measurements or observations, (2) experimental data from published studies, or (3) data from credible sources online. This may include tables, raw data, photos, sketches, or diagrams. Cite them appropriately.

  6. Modeling: Define the variables involved in the investigation. Construct equations, diagrams, tables, or representations showing the relationships between variables. Develop and present the final quadratic model of the phenomenon.

    At this part, you are allowed to use AI tools to assist you in constructing the model. However, you must declare the use of AI in the appendices signed by your teacher.

    For graphs, I recommend using Desmos as visual aid.

  7. Solution: Answer your investigation questions in detail. Use equations, computations, and visual aids like graphs or diagrams, and briefly interpret them.
  8. Conclusions: Summarize your findings. Answer your investigation questions directly. Offer some implications of your results. Also, provide recommendations to future investigators.
  9. References: List all references used in the investigation following APA 7 formatting guidelines.
  10. Appendices: Include here detailed tables, extended computations, large diagrams, questionnaires, and the declaration of AI use.
  11. Documentation: Include at least five (5) photographs documenting the group's investigation process. Provide short reflection statements describing your individual experiences.

Example Investigation Paper

Take note of the following formatting guidelines:

  1. Paper size is Letter (Short, 8.5 x 11 inches).
  2. Only use fonts Times New Roman or Latin Modern Roman in size 12.
  3. Set 1-inch margins on all sides.
  4. Use APA 7 formatting for in-text citations and references.
  5. AI usage threshold must be ≤ 25% and plagiarism ≤ 5%.
